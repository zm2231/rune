- **iOS: an app's other windows and its overlays show.** `rune::run_windows` and `rune::run` start every window an app has, not only the first: on an iPad whose bundle declares several scenes (`scripts/ios-app --multiple-scenes`) each window is a scene of its own, restored with its window at launch, and on an iPhone the others are page sheets over the first. `AppHandle` and `WindowHandle` show, hide, close and focus them, `Tx::close_window` closes one, a window keeps its state while hidden, and the user closing one runs its `App::on_close`. `App::overlay`'s overlays show over the window of the app that made them, placed from its safe area, fading and passing touches through as their `OverlayInput` says. `Tx::quit` and `Tx::relaunch` on iOS now log that they were ignored, as `AppHandle::quit` already did: an iOS app ends when the system ends it. A view out of its window gives back its renderer and drawables on a memory warning and makes them again when it next draws. Apps started with `run_windows` whose factory returns several windows now open them on iOS; an app that relied on them staying closed returns one window there.
