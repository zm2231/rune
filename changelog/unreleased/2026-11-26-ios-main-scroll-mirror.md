- **iOS: UIKit's bars follow a Rune view's main list.** A `RuneView` keeps a scroll view that fills it, draws nothing and takes no touch, and after each frame holds the main list's length and offset. Its nearest view controller gets it for each of the top and bottom edges that has no content scroll view of its own, and loses it when the view leaves. On the iOS 27 simulator, under SwiftUI's `NavigationStack`, the navigation bar blurred the Rune cards that scrolled under it, and a large title collapsed and opened with the list. A tap on the status bar asks this scroll view, which replaces the one-point strip that heard it before. `Runtime::main_scroll` reads the main list's offset and reach in the view's points; `Runtime::shift_main_scroll` moves the list by a change in the safe area's top, so its content stays put under the finger as a large title collapses. `examples/swiftui-host` puts a Rune list under SwiftUI's bars.
