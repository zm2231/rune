- **iOS: `El::spellcheck` marks misspelled words and a field's long-press menu offers their suggestions.** The checker is UIKit's `UITextChecker` behind the interface Windows and macOS use, so the markers, the menu's suggestions and Learn Spelling, `rune::spell::available_languages`, `set_languages` and the Spellcheck Languages menu work as on Windows: `UITextChecker` takes one language at a time, so a word is marked when every chosen dictionary calls it misspelled, and with none chosen the dictionaries are one per user language (`NSLocale.preferredLanguages`) that `UITextChecker.availableLanguages` has, plus US English when none is English. Words are checked on the main thread and their answers kept, at most 32 new words for each paint of a field, which asks for another frame until its text is whole, so a long text's marks fill in over frames; off the main thread the checker answers nothing. A word over 128 bytes is not checked, and a preferred language with no dictionary of its exact tag or bare language takes the first dictionary of that language (`es-419` takes `es-ES`). The marker is a row of dots, as on macOS. `web-hello`'s composer checks spelling. Shown on the iOS simulator in English (markers after an emoji and CJK text, a suggestion replacing its word, Learn Spelling); not yet verified on a device or in another language.
