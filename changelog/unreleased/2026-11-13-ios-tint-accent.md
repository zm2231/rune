- **iOS: the app's accent is its view's tint, so an app's default accent is now iOS's system blue instead of the built-in blue.** `Cx::accent`, `Cx::focus_color` and default focus rings were the built-in `#007AFF` (`#0A84FF` in dark) on iOS whatever the tint. A `RuneView` now gives its runtime its `tintColor`, resolved for a light and a dark appearance with the view's other traits, and focus rings are that colour at half its alpha. **Behavior change:** an app that sets no tint draws its accent in iOS's system blue, `#0088FF` (`#0091FF` in dark) as measured on the iOS 27.2 simulator. A view inherits its tint from the views above it, which is where a UIKit host's `tintColor` and a SwiftUI host's `.tint()` land. The accent is whatever UIKit reports as the view's tint, including its dimmed tint while an alert or sheet is over the view. It is read when the app starts and again when UIKit says the tint changed (`tintColorDidChange`) or the view's traits did (dark, Increase Contrast); the view redraws, memos included, only when the colours differ from the ones it had. A colour outside sRGB (Display P3) is clipped to sRGB, and a tint with no RGB form (a pattern) is the built-in default. `rune::system_accent()` has no view to read and still returns `SystemAccent::DEFAULT` on iOS: read the accent in a view with `Cx::accent` and `Cx::focus_color`. `Runtime::set_system_accent` now returns whether the accent changed.
