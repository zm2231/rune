- **Sideways scrolling shows what's past its edges, and tabs keep their titles at large text.** The new `rune_kit::ScrollOverflow` wraps an `El::scroll_x` viewport with a chevron over each edge that has content past it, on a backing in the surface colour (solid with a strong border under Increase Contrast); pressing one pages the viewport that way, gliding on the scroll spring or jumping under reduced motion, and holding it keeps paging. `ScrollOverflow::reveal` names an element the viewport opens on, scrolled into view on first layout and when the wrapper moves or resizes. A hidden chevron takes no presses, and the chevrons are pointer affordances outside the focus order and the accessibility tree, as keyboard focus already scrolls a focused element into view. A horizontal `TabStrip` shows them by default (`TabStrip::overflow_chevrons(false)` turns them off) and opens on its active tab. Above text scale 1 its tabs also stop shrinking at a readable width, ramping from the policy's `min_width` at 1.0 to `TabOverflow::TITLE_FLOOR` (96 points at text scale 1, scaled with the text) by 1.3, and the strip scrolls instead of dropping titles (a title longer than the floor leaves room for still fades at its end); `TabStrip::title_floor(None)` keeps the old shrinking, and `TabOverflow::with_title_floor` applies the floor to a policy. `SegmentedControl::scrolls` lets segments that don't fit scroll sideways with the chevrons, on by default for `as_tabs().fit()`, opening on the selected segment (the scroll viewport is keyed `segments`, and a key given to the built control lands on its wrapper); radio groups never scroll unless asked. At text scale 1 tab widths are unchanged, and a strip with nothing past its edges draws as before; its scroll viewport now sits inside a wrapper keyed `{key}-scroll-overflow`. `Tx::scroll_into_view_in` (and the other scoped scroll requests) also take an element's full tree path as the scope.
