- **iOS: a tap on the status bar scrolls the main list to its top.** The tap did nothing in a Rune view, which has no `UIScrollView` for UIKit to tell. A `RuneView` now keeps one for it to ask (a strip one point high across its top edge that takes no touches and is hidden from accessibility), and when UIKit asks, the new `Runtime::scroll_main_to_top` glides the main list to its top on the spring `Tx::scroll_to` uses: a long trip starts two viewports out, virtual rows mount as it goes, a touch or the wheel takes over mid-glide, and reduced motion jumps. The main list is the first vertical scroll viewport down the view's centre line from the status bar, so a list under a title bar is found below the bar; of columns side by side it is the one under the centre, of a pager's pages the one showing, with a modal open the modal's own list, and of nested viewports the outermost whose content overflows. The first viewport down the line ends the search even when its content fits, so a short conversation over a long composer scrolls nothing. A list at its top does nothing and draws no frame, and a `stick_bottom` list stops following its end. On the iOS simulator, UIKit asked every scroll view under the tap's x, including a host's own, which then scrolled too; a real tap on a device, and a Rune view in one column of a split view, are not yet verified.
